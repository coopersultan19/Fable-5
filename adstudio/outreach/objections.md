# Objection Handling — Word-for-Word

Say these nearly verbatim, then stop talking. The biggest objection-handling
mistake is the third sentence. Every answer ends by moving back to the only
question that matters: *want the ads or not?*

---

## "Is this AI?"

> Parts of the production pipeline, yeah — that's how ten ads happen in 48 hours
> instead of three weeks. The scripts, the hooks, and the compliance checks are
> me. But honestly, the fairest test is the one in your inbox: you've seen the
> ad I made for [product]. If it stops the scroll, it stops the scroll. Run it
> against whatever your current creative is doing and let the CTR argue with me.

*Never dodge, never be defensive, never volunteer it unprompted. Redirect to
the work — you're a studio judged on output, not on tooling.*

---

## "We already have a UGC agency."

> Keep them — seriously. UGC agencies are great at hero content. What they can't
> do is volume: you're getting what, 3–5 videos a month? That's not enough to
> test hooks properly. Brands use me as the testing layer: I flood the account
> with 10 angles for $497, you find the 2 that hit, then your UGC agency shoots
> the polished version of the winners. Your agency actually gets better because
> they stop guessing which concepts to film.

---

## "Too expensive."

> $497 is ten ads — that's $50 an ad. One UGC creator charges $150–$300 for a
> single video and takes two weeks. But price aside, here's the real math: if
> you're spending even $10k/month on Meta, one hook that lifts CTR twenty percent
> pays for the batch inside a week. And you've already seen the free one, so you
> know exactly what you're buying. If it's still a no, no hard feelings — the
> free ad is yours.

---

## "Send me examples."

> Already did — the [product] ad I sent *is* the example, made for your exact
> product, not a sizzle reel of someone else's brand. But sure, here's more:
> [portfolio link]. Fair warning, the most relevant example will always be the
> one with your product in it, and you're holding it.

*If they ask before you've sent the sample: make the sample first. Never send
a portfolio link as a substitute for the personalized ad — it converts at a
fraction of the rate.*

---

## "How do I know these convert?"

> You don't yet — and neither does anyone selling you creative, including UGC
> agencies with case studies from other people's brands. Nobody can promise a
> winner; anyone who does is lying to you. What I promise is ten real shots at
> a winner in 48 hours, cheap enough that the test itself is nearly free. Run
> the free ad I sent with $50 behind it. If it holds its own against your
> current stuff, the other nine are the easiest $497 you'll spend this quarter.

---

## "Are these compliant?"

> Every script goes through a supplement-specific checklist before you ever see
> it: structure/function claims only — "supports muscle recovery," never "treats
> anything" — no disease language, FTC disclosure-ready, and checked against the
> stuff that trips Meta and TikTok's supplement filters. I'll be straight with
> you: I'm a studio, not a law firm, so your final claim substantiation is your
> side of the table. But ad-account bans come from lazy scripting, and lazy
> scripting is the exact thing I exist to prevent.

---

## Bonus: "Can we hop on a call?"

> Happy to if you want one, but you probably don't need it — the whole process
> is: you pay, I send you five intake questions, ads arrive in 48 hours. Most
> founders would rather have the ads than the meeting. Here's the link: [Stripe
> link]. If anything feels unclear after the intake questions, I'll call you.

*Take the call if they insist — some retainer clients need voice contact to
trust you. But never require one.*
